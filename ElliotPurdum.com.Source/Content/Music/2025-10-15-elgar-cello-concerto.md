Edward Elgar's Cello Concerto in E minor, composed in 1919, sits at the emotional
heart of the cello repertoire — a work of autumnal weight, written in the shadow
of the First World War.

This performance with the Baldwin Wallace Symphony explored the concerto's
introspective character, especially in the slow movement, where the cello carries
an almost confessional weight against the orchestra's hushed accompaniment.
